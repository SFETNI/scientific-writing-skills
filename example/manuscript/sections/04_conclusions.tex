\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The test set $R^2$ of $0.847 \pm 0.031$ suggests that the GBR surrogate generalises
reasonably well to compositions not seen during training, given the limited dataset size
of 120 specimens.
The gap between training ($0.924$) and test ($0.847$) performance is consistent
with mild overfitting, which may be reduced by increasing the dataset size or
applying additional regularisation.

The dominance of filler content in the feature importance analysis is consistent
with effective medium theory predictions, which assign primary importance to the
volume fraction of the filler phase \citep{Chen2020_ILLUSTRATIVE}.
The secondary role of aspect ratio aligns with percolation-threshold arguments
for high-aspect-ratio fillers \citep{Liu2022_ILLUSTRATIVE}.

The observed performance degradation at high filler loadings
(Supplementary Section~S2) may be attributed to filler agglomeration,
which reduces the effective contact area between graphene platelets and the matrix.
This interpretation is speculative and should be investigated with targeted
experiments before being stated as a conclusion.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
This study is limited to the synthetic design space covered by the 120-specimen dataset.
The model cannot be expected to extrapolate reliably beyond the training distribution.
The feature importance estimates are specific to the GBR architecture and may not
reflect the physical importance of each variable in a broader context.
All data in this study are synthetic; the results have no direct applicability
to real graphene composite systems.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

A gradient boosting regressor surrogate model was developed for predicting the thermal
conductivity of graphene-reinforced polymer composites in a synthetic dataset.
The model achieved $R^2 = 0.847 \pm 0.031$ on the test set, outperforming
a linear regression baseline ($R^2 = 0.631 \pm 0.044$).
Filler content was identified as the dominant predictor (38.4\% feature importance).
These results demonstrate the feasibility of surrogate modelling as a screening tool
for composite property optimisation, within the limitations stated above.
