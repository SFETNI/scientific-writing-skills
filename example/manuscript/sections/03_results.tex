\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Model accuracy on the test set}
\label{sec:model_accuracy}

The GBR model achieved $R^2 = 0.847 \pm 0.031$ and
RMSE $= 0.143 \pm 0.018$~W~m$^{-1}$~K$^{-1}$ on the held-out test set
(Table~\ref{tab:performance}).
The mean absolute error was $0.112 \pm 0.014$~W~m$^{-1}$~K$^{-1}$.
The training set $R^2$ was $0.924 \pm 0.012$, indicating moderate overfitting
that remained within acceptable limits for a 120-sample dataset.

\subsection{Comparison to the linear regression baseline}
\label{sec:baseline}

The GBR model outperformed the linear regression baseline across all metrics
(Table~\ref{tab:performance}).
The baseline achieved $R^2 = 0.631 \pm 0.044$ on the test set,
compared to $0.847 \pm 0.031$ for GBR, corresponding to a difference of 0.216 in $R^2$.

\subsection{Feature importance}
\label{sec:feature_importance_results}

Filler content was identified as the dominant predictor,
with a mean decrease in impurity of $38.4\%$ across all 200 trees
(Figure~\ref{fig:feature_importance}).
Aspect ratio ranked second at $27.1\%$, followed by matrix thermal conductivity at $19.6\%$.
The remaining five variables collectively accounted for $14.9\%$ of total importance.

\subsection{Sensitivity analysis}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

The model prediction range across all specimens spanned
$0.21$--$3.84$~W~m$^{-1}$~K$^{-1}$,
consistent with the physical range reported for graphene-filled polymer composites
in the synthetic design space (Supplementary Table~S1).
